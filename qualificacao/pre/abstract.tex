\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
This qualifying text examines land use and land cover dynamics in the state of
Goiás, Brazil, between 1985 and 2024, and their relationship with socioeconomic
factors, using the annual MapBiomas series (Collection 10.1) for the 246
municipalities of Goiás and a set of 166 Minimum Comparable Areas that keeps
territory constant in longitudinal analyses. A data-driven periodization
divides the 40 years into three acts (pasture as legacy, 1985--2000; expansion
and intensification, 2001--2019; and accelerated conversion under an ambiguous
label, 2020--2024) and frames the central finding: a northward spatial
reorganization of land conversion within the state (``the northward march'').
The investigation unfolds along four fronts: the existence of the pattern
(displacement of the center of mass of each land use, which survives checks on
pixel, spatial unit, class and error bar, with a latitudinal gradient that
persists across the forty years); the local mechanism, in which two logics
coexist throughout the state and the region explains little of the age of the
converted pasture, even though the dominant transition runs from pasture to
cropland in the South and from natural vegetation to pasture in the North; a
formal test of intra-state causal displacement, which is not confirmed and whose
alternative reading is a coordinated response to a common macroeconomic driver,
distributed along the South--North gradient (corroborating, not established,
evidence); and the recent deceleration, consistent with the exhaustion of land
still exposed to conversion in the South, supported both by the elimination of
competing hypotheses and by a measured mechanism, the decline in the conversion
rate as the region is depleted. The text presents the methodology, preliminary
results, the limits of what the work claims, and the schedule until the
defense.

\textbf{Keywords}: land use and land cover; Cerrado; agricultural frontier;
Goiás; MapBiomas.
\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
